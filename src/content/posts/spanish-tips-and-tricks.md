You've decided to learn Spanish. Excellent choice. Now the question is: how do you actually make it stick without burning out after two weeks?

These tips are not theory — they are practical moves you can start using today.

---

## 1. Talk to Yourself in Spanish

Sounds strange? It works. Narrate your day in Spanish as you go about your routine. Making coffee? *"Estoy haciendo café."* Getting dressed? *"Me estoy vistiendo."* This builds the habit of thinking in Spanish rather than translating from English, which is the single biggest leap you can make.

---

## 2. Use Spaced Repetition (Not Random Review)

Apps like Anki or the built-in system in Duolingo use **spaced repetition** — showing you words just before you'd forget them. This is scientifically proven to be dramatically more effective than reviewing everything every day. Use a deck, do 15–20 cards per day, and trust the algorithm.

---

## 3. Learn Phrases, Not Just Words

Instead of memorizing *hablar* (to speak) in isolation, learn it inside a phrase: *"¿Puedo hablar más despacio?"* (Can I speak more slowly?). Phrases give your brain context, which makes them far easier to recall under pressure.

---

## 4. Watch TV in Spanish — With Spanish Subtitles

Here's the key most people miss: don't use English subtitles. Use **Spanish subtitles**. Your brain will connect the written and spoken form simultaneously. Start with shows you already know well (so you don't need to follow the plot) and work up to new content. *La Casa de Papel*, *Club de Cuervos*, and *Extra en Español* are great starting points.

---

## 5. Embrace the False Friends (and Learn from Them)

False friends are words that look like English words but mean something completely different. For example:

- **Embarazada** does NOT mean "embarrassed" — it means **pregnant**.
- **Éxito** does NOT mean "exit" — it means **success**.
- **Constipado** does NOT mean "constipated" — it means **having a cold**.

These are funny, memorable, and once you learn them, you never forget them. Make a list.

---

## 6. Find a Language Partner, Not Just an App

Apps are useful tools, but they can't replace real conversation. Platforms like **Tandem** or **HelloTalk** connect you with native Spanish speakers who want to practice English. You help them, they help you — it's free, it's motivating, and it's real.

---

## 7. Change Your Phone to Spanish

This one is painless and surprisingly effective. Go to settings right now and switch your phone's language to Spanish. You already know where everything is, so you won't be lost — but every notification, every menu, every button label becomes passive input. After a week, you'll be reading Spanish without thinking about it.

---

## 8. Master the 100 Most Common Words First

The most frequent 100 words in Spanish make up about 50% of everyday speech. These include words like *ser/estar* (to be), *tener* (to have), *hacer* (to do/make), and common prepositions and conjunctions.

Don't try to learn everything at once. Get these 100 into your muscle memory first, and everything else builds on top of them.

---

## 9. Sing Along to Spanish Music

Music encodes language into long-term memory better than almost anything else. Pick songs you enjoy — reggaeton, flamenco, Latin pop — look up the lyrics, read along while listening, and then try to sing along. You'll be surprised how quickly those lines become automatic.

Artists to start with: **Bad Bunny**, **Shakira**, **Juanes**, **Rosalía**, **Alejandro Sanz**.

---

## 10. Make Peace with Not Understanding Everything

The number one reason people quit Spanish is the frustration of not understanding native speakers at full speed. Here's the truth: even intermediate learners only catch 60–70% of fast conversation. That's normal. That's the process.

The goal isn't to understand every word — it's to understand enough to keep going. Let unfamiliar words wash over you. Over time, the fog clears.

---

## The Real Trick

Consistency beats intensity every time. Twenty minutes a day for a year will get you much further than an eight-hour weekend binge followed by a two-week break.

Treat Spanish like a daily habit — like brushing your teeth — and one day you'll wake up and realize you're actually doing it.

*¡Tú puedes!* (You can do it!)
